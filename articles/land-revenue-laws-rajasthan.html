Land Revenue Laws in Rajasthan: Mutation, Khasra & Revenue Court Proceedings

By Shubham Ojha, BA LL.B., LL.M., ICWA (Foundation) | Advocate, Rajasthan High Court, Jodhpur April 2026

In short: two Rajasthan Acts govern agricultural land. The Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 creates the revenue officers, the record of rights and the appeal ladder that ends at the Board of Revenue at Ajmer. The Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 creates khatedari rights and sends every suit in its Third Schedule to a revenue court, not a civil court. Mutation is a record entry under Sections 133 to 135 of the 1956 Act. It is not title.

Two Acts, two jobs

People say "revenue law" as if it were one thing. It is two statutes. They do different work. Most wrong filings come from mixing them up.

The Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 is about the machine. Section 4 establishes the Board. Section 20 tells the State to appoint a Collector in every district and a Tehsildar in every tehsil, and allows Assistant Collectors, Sub-Divisional Officers and Naib-Tehsildars. Chapter VII (Sections 106 to 141) is the record of rights. Sections 74 to 87 are appeals, revision and review. Section 90-A is conversion of agricultural land. Section 91 is summary eviction of a trespasser from government land. That is the whole machine.

The Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 is about the rights in the land. Section 15 says who is a khatedar tenant. Sections 39 to 43 say how that interest passes by will, by succession, by sale and by mortgage. Section 88 gives the suit for declaration of tenancy. Section 207 says every suit or application listed in the Third Schedule shall be heard by a revenue court and by no other court. Section 242 tells a civil court what to do when a plea of tenancy is raised before it.

The records: khewat, khatauni, khasra, jamabandi

Section 113 of the 1956 Act requires the Land Records Officer to frame a record of rights for every village. Under Section 3(i) the Land Records Officer is the Collector. Section 114 lists what the record contains: a khewat, the register of estate-holders; a khatauni, the register of all persons cultivating or otherwise holding land; a register of rent-free holders; and other prescribed registers. Section 121 lists what the khatauni must say about each tenant: the class of his tenure under the Tenancy Act, the date of his khatedari parcha, every transfer he has made with its particulars, the khasra number and area of each field in his holding, and the annual rent.

The jamabandi that villagers speak of is the annual register of Section 132. The Land Records Officer maintains the record of rights by preparing a set, or an amended set, of these registers "annually or at such longer intervals as the State Government may prescribe", and records in them every change that affects a recorded right. The Act does not say "every five years". That is a settlement habit. It is not the statute.

Three sections make the record usable. Section 138: all maps, field books and registers are open to public inspection free of cost. Section 139: the Patwari issues attested copies on payment of the prescribed fee. Section 140: every entry in the record of rights "shall be presumed to be true until the contrary is proved". That last clause is why a jamabandi is evidence, and why it is not proof of ownership. The Supreme Court said the same in Balwant Singh v. Daulat Singh (1997): revenue entries serve fiscal purposes and neither create nor extinguish title. The digitised copy on the Apna Khata portal (apnakhata.raj.nic.in) carries the same presumption and the same limit.

Mutation in the Act, not in folklore

Mutation (intkal, dakhil-kharij) is three sections long. Read them once. Section 133: every person obtaining possession of land by succession, transfer or otherwise shall bring the fact to the notice of the village Patwari and report it to the Tehsildar within three months of obtaining possession; a guardian reports for a minor. Section 134: neglect to report attracts a fine of up to ten rupees. Section 135: the Tehsildar makes such inquiry as appears necessary; in an undisputed case he records the change in the annual register; in a disputed case he decides the dispute if competent, and if not, refers it to the competent officer.

Section 136 lets the Land Records Officer correct clerical errors, and errors the interested parties admit, at any time, with a show-cause notice where the error was found on inspection. Section 23(2) read with item (7) of the First Schedule makes "mutation upon succession, transfer or otherwise" a judicial matter, which is why a mutation order can be appealed and revised. The full procedure, with documents for each kind of transfer, is on the page on mutation in Rajasthan (Hindi).

Who sits where

  • Patwari (Sections 30, 31): keeps the village papers, prepares the field report, issues copies under Section 139.
  • Tehsildar (Section 20(a)(ii)): the mutation officer under Section 135; summary eviction of trespassers from government land under Section 91; the court of first instance for several Third Schedule suits.
  • Sub-Divisional Officer: an Assistant Collector in charge of one or more sub-divisions (Section 20(c)(i)); under Section 24(iii) all Tehsildars in the sub-division are subordinate to him. Section 5(40) of the Tenancy Act defines the term the same way.
  • Collector (Section 20(a)(i)): also the Land Records Officer for the district. First appellate court from a Tehsildar's original order under Section 75(1)(a); revisional power to call for records under Section 82.
  • Revenue Appellate Authority (Section 20-A): hears appeals from Assistant Collectors, Sub-Divisional Officers and Collectors under Section 75(1)(b). Section 56-A allows such an appeal to be presented to the Collector of the district where the cause of action arose.
  • Board of Revenue: headquarters at Ajmer under Section 6; general superintendence over every revenue court and officer under Section 9. It sits as a single member or a Bench (Section 10). A party aggrieved by a single member's decision has a special appeal to a Bench within one month, if the member declares the case fit for appeal. Under Section 12 a Bench may refer a question of public importance to the High Court.
  • Rajasthan High Court, Jodhpur: no appeal lies from the Board under the 1956 Act. The remedy is a writ under Articles 226 and 227 of the Constitution. Under Section 231 of the Tenancy Act the High Court may also call for the record of a revenue case in which an appeal lies to a civil court under Section 239 but none to the High Court.

Section 56 allows every appearance before a revenue court to be made by the party, by a recognised agent, or by a legal practitioner. Section 57 gives the court power to summon witnesses and documents, and Section 57(4) a bailable warrant for a person who ignores the summons. These are courts. They have the powers of courts.

Appeals, revision, review: the clocks

Section 74 opens the chapter: no appeal lies from a revenue order except as provided in the Act. Section 75 gives the first appeal, Section 76 the second. Section 77 bars appeals from interim orders and from orders rejecting a revision or review. The limitation is in Section 78. It is short:

  • to the Collector, Land Records Officer or Settlement Officer: 30 days from the order;
  • to the Revenue Appellate Authority, Settlement Commissioner or Director of Land Records: 60 days;
  • to the Board: 90 days.

Section 228 of the Tenancy Act sets the same three periods for appeals in tenancy suits: 30 days to the Collector, 60 to the Revenue Appellate Authority, 90 to the Board. Section 79 of the 1956 Act requires a certified copy of the order with the appeal. Section 80 lets the appellate authority admit, summarily reject after hearing, confirm, vary, reverse, take further evidence or remand. Section 81 allows a stay of execution once the appeal is admitted, and Section 81(2) lets the officer who passed the order stay it himself before the appeal period runs out. Section 87 applies the Limitation Act. A delay application is possible. The cause must be real.

Where no appeal lies, Section 84 gives the Board revision over any judicial case in which the lower court exercised a jurisdiction not vested in it, failed to exercise one that was, or acted illegally or with material irregularity. Section 84A bars revision against interim orders. Section 86(2) allows every revenue court to review its own or a predecessor's order; where the order affects a right between private persons the application must be filed within 90 days and must rest on an Order 47 Rule 1 CPC ground. Section 85 forbids any revisional order to a person's prejudice without hearing him.

Khatedari rights under the Tenancy Act

Section 15(1) makes a khatedar tenant of every person who was a tenant at the commencement of the Act otherwise than as a sub-tenant or tenant of khudkasht, every person later admitted as a tenant, every allottee under rules made under Section 101 of the 1956 Act, and everyone who acquires khatedari under the Act or another law. The proviso denies khatedari to land let temporarily in the Gang Canal, Bhakra, Chambal or Jawai project areas or any notified area, and Section 15A does the same for the Indira Gandhi Canal area. Section 16 lists land in which khatedari does not accrue.

Once acquired, the interest behaves like property. It passes. It sells. It can be mortgaged. Section 39: a khatedar may bequeath it by will under his personal law. Section 40: on intestacy it devolves by the personal law applicable at death, which for a Hindu family means the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, daughters included. Section 41: it is transferable otherwise than by sub-lease, subject to Sections 42 and 43. Section 42(b): a sale, gift or bequest by a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe to a person outside that class is void; clause (bb) adds the Saharia tribe. Section 43: a khatedar may mortgage his interest to obtain a loan. Section 37 says the rights of a tenant in a holding are not liable to seizure, attachment or sale by process of any civil court.

Section 88 gives the suits: a person claiming to be a tenant or co-tenant may sue for a declaration of his tenancy or his share. Section 89 adds suits as to the class of tenancy, the area and boundaries of the holding, and the rent. Section 214 applies the limitation written in the Third Schedule to each of them, and Section 215 the court fee in its sixth column. The Schedule also names the court for each suit, which is why the first question at a consultation is "which entry of the Third Schedule is this".

Conversion and encroachment

Section 90-A of the 1956 Act: no person holding land for agriculture, and no transferee of it, shall use it for construction or any other non-agricultural purpose except with the written permission of the State Government, obtained in the prescribed manner. A house on a khatedari plot without conversion is a breach of Section 90-A. The jamabandi does not cure it. Section 91: a person occupying government land without lawful authority is a trespasser and may be summarily evicted by the Tehsildar, on his own motion or on the application of the local authority to which the land is placed. The crop, building or deposit on such land is liable to forfeiture if not removed within the time the Tehsildar fixes.

Which forum at Jodhpur for which problem

  1. A name has to change after a death or sale: Tehsildar, Section 135, within the three-month clock of Section 133.
  2. A clerical slip in the khatauni: Land Records Officer (the Collector), Section 136.
  3. Someone denies that you are a tenant, or disputes your share: declaratory suit under Section 88 of the Tenancy Act in the revenue court named in the Third Schedule.
  4. Division of a joint khatedari holding: suit under Section 53 of the Tenancy Act before the Assistant Collector.
  5. Ownership itself is disputed (forged deed, benami, adverse possession): civil court. If a tenancy plea arises there, Section 242 sends that issue alone to the revenue court and the civil court decides the suit on its finding.
  6. A wrong order: appeal within 30, 60 or 90 days under Section 78; then revision under Section 84; then a writ at the Jodhpur Bench.
  7. Bank has issued a SARFAESI notice on mortgaged land: neither forum above. Section 17 of the SARFAESI Act takes the matter to the Debts Recovery Tribunal, which for Rajasthan sits at Jaipur. See what to do after a Section 13(2) notice.

Papers to bring to a first meeting

  • The current jamabandi copy from Apna Khata and, if possible, the earlier settlement copy; the two are compared line by line. Differences matter.
  • The khasra numbers and the village map (naksha) for the fields in dispute.
  • Every deed in the chain: sale deed, gift deed, will, partition deed, with registration particulars.
  • The mutation order or the order under challenge, with the date of its certified copy; Section 78 counts from the order.
  • Any notice received from the Tehsil, the Collector or a bank.
  • Death certificates and the family tree where succession is in issue.

Heirs living outside India: where a khatedar dies and one of the heirs is abroad, the mutation is usually applied for by whoever stayed behind, and the jamabandi settles into a single name long before anyone overseas learns of it. The revenue procedure itself is no different, but the limitation and power-of-attorney questions that follow are. Those are taken up in securing inherited land in Rajasthan from abroad.

Which of these steps is taken before the Tehsildar at Jodhpur, which climbs to the Board of Revenue at Ajmer, and which ends as a writ before the Jodhpur Bench is set out together with the chamber's own particulars (enrolment, address and the hours it is open) on the page for an advocate in Jodhpur at the Rajasthan High Court.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between khasra, khatauni and jamabandi?

Under Section 121 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 the khasra number identifies each field and its area. The khatauni (Section 114(b)) is the register of every person cultivating or holding land in the village, with the class of tenure, transfers and rent against each name. The jamabandi is the annual register of Section 132 in which the record of rights is maintained and every change is entered.

Does a mutation entry make me the owner of the land?

No. Section 140 says an entry in the record of rights is presumed true until the contrary is proved; it is a rebuttable presumption, not title. In Balwant Singh v. Daulat Singh (1997) the Supreme Court held that revenue entries are for fiscal purposes and do not create or extinguish title. Ownership is decided by a civil court.

Within how many days must a mutation be reported?

Section 133 requires every person obtaining possession by succession, transfer or otherwise to inform the village Patwari and report to the Tehsildar within three months. Section 134 provides a fine of up to ten rupees for neglect; the practical risk is that another claimant records his name first.

Where does an appeal against a revenue order go, and within what time?

Section 75 gives the first appeal, generally from a Tehsildar to the Collector and from an Assistant Collector, Sub-Divisional Officer or Collector to the Revenue Appellate Authority; Section 76 gives the second appeal, ending at the Board of Revenue at Ajmer. Section 78 fixes the limitation at 30 days to the Collector, 60 days to the Revenue Appellate Authority and 90 days to the Board, counted from the order. A certified copy must accompany the appeal under Section 79.

Can a civil court decide a dispute about khatedari rights?

Not directly. Section 207 of the Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955 reserves every suit in the Third Schedule to a revenue court, and the declaratory suit on tenancy is under Section 88. Where a tenancy plea arises in a civil suit, Section 242 requires the civil court to frame that issue and send it to the revenue court, then decide the suit on the finding returned.

Can agricultural land in Rajasthan be used for building a house?

Only with written permission under Section 90-A of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956, obtained in the prescribed manner from the prescribed officer. Construction without it is a breach of the section regardless of the jamabandi entry, and government land occupied without authority is open to summary eviction by the Tehsildar under Section 91.
