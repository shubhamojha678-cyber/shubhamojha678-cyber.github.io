Work at the Rajasthan High Court, Principal Seat, Jodhpur

Most of the chamber’s work is at the Jodhpur Bench, and it falls into a few kinds. A petition under Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 to quash an FIR or a pending proceeding. Bail, usually once the Sessions Court has said no. An appeal under Section 14A(2) of the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989, where a Special Court has refused bail; that is an appeal, not a fresh application, and it has its own clock. Writ petitions under Articles 226 and 227 against service orders, revenue orders and the orders of local authorities. And contempt, when an order of this Court is simply not obeyed.

The order sheet decides the order of work. The first thing read in any High Court matter is the last order below it and its date, because the limitation runs from there. Everything else waits on that date.

Work in the courts at Jodhpur

Below the High Court the chamber takes matters in the District and Sessions Court, Jodhpur, and the courts under it. Sessions-triable matters start there, and so do criminal appeals and revisions from the magistrates. Complaints for a dishonoured cheque under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 are tried by the Judicial Magistrate. Maintenance under Section 144 BNSS and divorce and matrimonial cases go to the Family Court. Eviction under the Rajasthan Rent Control Act, 2001 goes to the Rent Tribunal, and accident claims to the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal.

Agricultural land is different. Partition of khatedari land is a revenue suit under Section 53 of the Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955, and mutation is decided by the revenue officers, not a civil court. The revenue ladder itself, in Hindi — Tehsildar, Assistant Collector, the Revenue Appellate Authority, the Board of Revenue at Ajmer, and the thirty, sixty and ninety-day appeal periods under Section 78 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 — is set out on जोधपुर में सम्पत्ति और राजस्व के मामले. The criminal ladder at Jodhpur and the full map of which court in Jodhpur hears which matter are set out on their own pages.

Two papers that belong somewhere else

Both are often taken to be Jodhpur matters. Neither is heard here. A bank’s possession notice under the SARFAESI Act, 2002 is challenged by an application under Section 17 before the Debts Recovery Tribunal at Jaipur, within forty-five days of the measure. A revenue matter that has passed the Revenue Appellate Authority goes on to the Board of Revenue at Ajmer. A petition filed in the wrong place costs days that a forty-five-day limit cannot spare.

Service disputes are the opposite case. A Central Government employee’s matter goes to the Central Administrative Tribunal, Jodhpur Bench, which does sit here. So does the Rajasthan High Court’s principal seat, and a State employee’s service order is usually challenged there, by a writ petition, or before the Rajasthan Civil Services Appellate Tribunal.

What to bring

Bring paper, not a summary of it. What helps depends on the matter:

  • Criminal: the FIR, the latest order sheet, and any bail order already passed or refused. If someone is in custody, the date of the first remand, because the sixty and ninety days under Section 187(3) BNSS are counted from it.
  • Land: the jamabandi, the khasra numbers, the mutation order or its rejection, and the sale deed or the family partition paper.
  • Bank notice: the Section 13(2) demand notice, the 13(4) possession notice if it has come, and the date each one was received.
  • Family: proof of the marriage, any order of the Family Court or the Magistrate, and income papers where maintenance is in issue.
  • Service: the order complained of, the charge-sheet or show-cause notice, and an extract of the service book if one can be had.

Phone photographs are enough for a first reading. Originals can wait. Certified copies cannot. Where one is prescribed, the Jodhpur Bench registry will return a petition that annexes a plain photocopy, so the application for it goes in early.

From outside Jodhpur, or outside India

Section 30 of the Advocates Act, 1961 lets an enrolled advocate appear in any court in India. The bench is fixed by the district where the case arose, so a family in Mumbai with an FIR registered in Pali still has a Jodhpur Bench matter. Land works the same way: a field in Barmer stays a Barmer revenue matter wherever its owner now lives. Papers can be sent as photographs on WhatsApp and discussed by telephone or video; see how a consultation works and working from outside India. Where a party already has counsel elsewhere, drafting alone is also taken. A payment sent from abroad to an exporter here reads differently again, because the first question is who the money actually went to: where an overseas buyer whose advance brought no goods can take that dispute.

Which court at Jodhpur hears a revenue appeal?

That turns on who passed the order and whether it was an original order or one already passed in appeal. Under Section 75 of the Rajasthan Land Revenue Act, 1956 a first appeal from a Tehsildar’s original order lies to the Collector; from an original order of an Assistant Collector, a Sub-Divisional Officer or the Collector, to the Revenue Appellate Authority; and from an original order of the Revenue Appellate Authority, to the Board of Revenue. Where the order under challenge was itself passed in appeal, Section 76 takes a second appeal from the Collector to the Revenue Appellate Authority and from the Revenue Appellate Authority to the Board. Tenancy suits run a parallel ladder of their own under Sections 223 and 224 of the Rajasthan Tenancy Act, 1955, set out on जोधपुर में सम्पत्ति और राजस्व के मामले. The periods in Section 78 are what lapses quietly: thirty days to the Collector, sixty days to the Revenue Appellate Authority, ninety days to the Board, counted from the date of the order objected to. Section 79 wants a certified copy of that order with the appeal unless its production is dispensed with, so the copy application is moved first and not last.

Which court at Jodhpur hears which matter?

It turns on the matter, not the party. Writ petitions under Articles 226 and 227, petitions under Section 528 BNSS, bail after a Sessions refusal, and appeals from the Family Court go to the Rajasthan High Court, Principal Seat, Jodhpur. Sessions-triable cases, criminal appeals from the magistrates and anticipatory bail ordinarily begin in the District and Sessions Court. Matrimonial and maintenance matters go to the Family Court, and khatedari land to the revenue courts.

Jodhpur ka vakil kaun-si court me jata hai?

Mamla tay karta hai, party nahin. Rajasthan High Court ki mukhya peeth (principal seat) Jodhpur me hi hai, aur Article 226 ya 227 ki writ, Section 528 BNSS ki quashing petition, aur Sessions se jamanat kharij hone ke baad ki bail wahin lagti hai. Sessions-triable mukadme, magistrate ke faisle ki appeal aur revision District and Sessions Court, Jodhpur me. Shaadi, talaq aur bharan-poshan Family Court me. Khetihar zameen revenue court me. Faujdari me Section 197 BNSS ka niyam seedha hai: jis court ke local jurisdiction me apradh hua, wahi use sunta hai. Civil me zameen ya makan ka mukadma Section 16 CPC ke tahat wahin lagta hai jahan property sthit hai, maalik chahe kahin aur rehta ho.

Vakil se milne kya le jana hai?

Zameen me jamabandi, khasra number aur namantaran (mutation) ka order ya uski rejection. Bank notice me Section 13(2) ka notice aur jis din wo mila uski tareekh. Pehli baar ke liye phone ki photo kaafi hai; asal kagaz baad me chalega, par certified copy wali arzi jaldi lagti hai.
